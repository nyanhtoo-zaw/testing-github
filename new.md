## Pillar 1: The Legal Shift (Compliance)

Jiwon / Nasser / Sayed / Nyan

> Think about a civil engineer designing a bridge. They are not just drawing a blueprint, they are accepting legal responsibility for its safety.  
>  
> Now compare that to software development. Historically, we have had the luxury of patch it later. But in 2026, that changes.  
>  
> With the full implementation of the EU Cyber Resilience Act, developers face strict legal obligations. If a critical vulnerability is found, it must be reported within 24 hours.  
>  
> This means security is no longer a final polish. It is a legal requirement from the start.

## Pillar 2: Deceptive Design (Dark Patterns)

Jiwon / Nasser / Sayed / Nyan

> While Pillar 1 focuses on the law, Pillar 2 focuses on the user.  
>  
> In modern development, we often see the weaponization of cognitive psychology, known as dark patterns.  
>  
> One of the most notorious is the Roach Motel. It is easy to enter a service but intentionally hard to leave.  
>  
> You have probably seen this through hidden unsubscribe buttons, confusing menus, or default settings that favor data collection.  
>  
> According to the 2024 Global Privacy Sweep by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, a significant number of platforms still rely on these deceptive designs, making it harder for users to make informed choices.  
>  
> By 2026, the ethical standard has shifted. Designs must follow symmetry of choice. The path to reject must be just as clear and accessible as accept.

## Pillar 3: Environmental Impact (Carbon Aware Computing)

Jiwon / Nasser / Sayed / Nyan

> Pillar 3 is about the environmental impact of our code.  
>  
> In the past, performance was all about speed. But in 2026, it is measured by energy efficiency.  
>  
> Every unnecessary CPU cycle means more carbon emissions. That is why we now talk about carbon aware computing.  
>  
> Research such as the Pereira study shows a huge gap in energy usage between languages.  
> For example, unoptimized Python can use up to 75 times more energy than Rust or C for the same task.  
>  
> So as developers, our choice of stack is not just technical, it is environmental.  
>  
> Optimization is not just about performance anymore. It is about sustainability.
